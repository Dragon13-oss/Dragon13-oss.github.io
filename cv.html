Statement

Research Positioning

My work investigates how media design can translate invisible states into perceivable experiences. I am especially interested in how physiological data, AI-supported interpretation, immersive environments, and visual systems can support human perception, reflection, and decision-making. Rather than treating design only as visual output, I approach it as a research method for building relationships between body, data, interface, and context.

My current projects explore care-oriented interaction, VR-based embodied training, multimodal signal visualization, and wearable biofeedback interfaces. Each project treats design as a prototype for understanding — building functional systems that ask questions about how humans perceive, respond to, and make sense of their own bodily states through media.

For my graduate study, I want to continue exploring how bodily signals can become visual and spatial interfaces, connecting wearable sensing, biofeedback, and perceptual media design.
