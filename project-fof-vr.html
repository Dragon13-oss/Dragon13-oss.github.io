Project Origin

This project began from a personal observation of my grandmother's experience with mobility and fear of falling. After falling down the stairs in her fifties, her leg strength and walking confidence gradually declined. She used to enjoy walking outdoors, but over time she became less willing to go out because of her unstable mobility and fear of falling.

“Fear was not only about falling again — it gradually changed the willingness to move.”

When I later encountered the concept of Fear of Falling, I understood this experience not only as a physical problem, but as a self-reinforcing loop between anxiety, avoidance, reduced movement, and declining confidence. This became the starting point of the project: how can VR provide safe, controllable, and repeatable embodied experiences that help older adults rebuild movement confidence?

Problem

Fear of Falling as a Self-Reinforcing Loop

Fear of falling gradually reduces mobility, confidence, and social participation — creating a self-reinforcing loop of avoidance and physical decline. When movement is avoided rather than safely re-experienced, the loop becomes stronger.

The design opportunity: how can VR provide safe, controllable, and repeatable embodied experiences that rebuild confidence through action?

Design Strategy

Three Design Principles

01

Safe Exposure

VR scenes show perceptual risk while the user remains physically safe in a standing zone.

→ Standing zone + virtual edge scenes

02

Repeated Bodily Success

Each task requires the body to act — stand, step, gaze, confirm. Small successes accumulate into confidence.

→ Gaze lock + trigger confirmation

03

Movement Confidence

Every completed action receives immediate visual feedback. Progress is recorded and visible.

→ Completion UI + progress record

Progressive Training Modules

Selected VR Training Modules

Four training modes with increasing perceptual challenge. Users progress only after achieving small successes at each level.

Surface Training

Stable ground, basic gaze targets. Building initial comfort with the environment.

Vertical Training

Height exposure with step confirmation. Controlled perceptual risk.

Dynamic Training

Moving pedestrians, attention division. Environmental complexity and timing.

Perceptual Training

Low light, depth cues, spatial judgment. Sensory and cognitive challenge.

Interaction Design

Action Before Interpretation

The body commits before the action executes. Each interaction turns confidence-building into concrete physical actions with immediate feedback.

Gaze Lock + Trigger Hold

User gazes at the target ring and holds the trigger to confirm readiness before stepping. Reduces anxiety from sudden movement.

Completion Feedback

Every completed task shows explicit confirmation. Bodily action becomes perceived progress.
